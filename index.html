04 — The evolution

From a brick on a street corner
to the trading desk in your pocket.

1973

The first call

Motorola's Martin Cooper walks down a New York street and makes the first public handheld cellular call. Humanity has not hung up since.

1983

DynaTAC 8000X — "The Brick"

Ten hours to charge, thirty minutes of talk, about $3,995 at launch. A status symbol you could also use to defend yourself.

1996

The flip

Hanging up became an art form. Snapping it shut was the most satisfying sound of the decade.

2000

The indestructible one

A battery that lasted a week, a case that survived a war, and a game about a snake. Peak civilisation.

2004

The razor-thin flex

Paper-thin, metal, impossibly cool. The first phone people bought purely to be seen holding it.

2000s

The keyboard years

Email on the go turned into email all the time. The first phone addiction got its own nickname. Thumbs got stronger.

2007

"Apple reinvents the phone."

Steve Jobs walks on stage with an iPod, a phone and an internet communicator — in one device. The keyboard dies. The touchscreen era begins.

2010s

Everyone. Everywhere.

App stores, front cameras, selfies, push notifications. Even the most powerful office on Earth huddles around a 3.5-inch screen.
